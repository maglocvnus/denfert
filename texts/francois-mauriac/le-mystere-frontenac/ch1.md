# Le Mystère Frontenac – I

<a id="p1"></a>
> XAVIER FRONTENAC [jeta](../../../verbs/j/je/jet/jeter.md) un [regard](../../../nouns/r/re/reg/regard.md) [timide](../../../adjectives/t/ti/tim/timide.md) sur sa belle-sœur qui [tricotait](../../../verbs/t/tr/tri/tricoter.md), le buste droit, sans s’[appuyer](../../../verbs/a/ap/app/appuyer.md) au dossier de la chaise basse qu’elle avait [rapprochée](../../../verbs/r/ra/rap/rapprocher.md) du feu ; et il [comprit](../../../verbs/c/co/com/comprendre.md) qu’elle était irritée. Il [chercha](../../../verbs/c/ch/che/chercher.md) à se [rappeler](../../../verbs/r/ra/rap/rappeler.md) ce qu’il avait [dit](../../../verbs/d/di/dir/dire.md), pendant le dîner : et ses propos lui semblèrent dénués de toute malice. Xavier [soupira](../../../verbs/s/so/sou/soupirer.md), [passa](../../../verbs/p/pa/pas/passer.md) sur son [crâne](../../../nouns/c/cr/cra/crâne.md) une [main](../../../nouns/m/ma/mai/main.md) [fluette](../../../adjectives/f/fl/flu/fluet.md).


Xavier Frontenac cast a timid glance at his sister-in-law, who was knitting, sitting bolt upright without leaning against the back of the low chair she had drawn close to the fire; and he realised that she was annoyed. He tried to remember what he had said during dinner, but his remarks seemed to him entirely innocent. Xavier sighed and ran a slender hand over his bald head.

<a id="p2"></a>
> Ses yeux fixèrent le grand lit à colonnes torses où, huit ans plus tôt, son frère aîné, Michel Frontenac, avait souffert cette interminable agonie. Il revit l tête renversée, le cou énorme, que dévorait la jeune barbe vigoureuse ; les mouches inlassables de juin qu’il ne pouvait chasser de cette face suante. Aujourd’hui, on aurait tenté de ke trépaner, on l’aurait sauvé peut-être ; Michel serait là. Il serait là... Xavier ne pouvait plus détourner les yeux de ce lit ni de ces murs. Pourtant ce n’était pas dans cet appartement que son frère avait expiré : huit jours après les obsèques, Blanche Frontenac, avec ses cinq enfants, avait quitté la maison de la rue Vital-Carles, et s’était réfugiée au troisième étage de l’hôtel qu’habitait, rue de Cursol, sa mère, Mme Arnaud-Miqueu.  

His eyes settled on the great four-poster bed with its twisted columns, where, eight years earlier, his elder brother, Michel Frontenac, had endured that interminable death agony. He saw again the head thrown back, the enormous neck engulfed by the thick growth of a vigorous young beard; the tireless June flies that he had been unable to brush away from that sweating face.

Nowadays, they would have attempted to operate on his skull; perhaps they would have saved him. Michel would be here. He would be here...

Xavier could no longer tear his eyes away from the bed or from those walls. And yet it was not in this apartment that his brother had breathed his last: eight days after the funeral, Blanche Frontenac, with her five children, had left the house in the Rue Vital-Carles and taken refuge on the third floor of the townhouse in the Rue de Cursol where her mother, Madame Arnaud-Miqueu, lived.
